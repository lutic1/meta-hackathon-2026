# Keyframe: Experience Spine

Luis Ticas · 2026-10-21

This spine covers how Keyframe behaves: its surfaces, copy, states, interactions, accessibility floor and journeys. `DESIGN.md` covers how it looks, and this file refers to its tokens by name (`{components.hotspot}`, `{colors.lamp}`). Requirements are cited by id (FR-n, NFR-n, UJ-n from the PRD; AD-n from the architecture spine) and are not restated. Where this spine had to infer something (timings, labels, client routes, draft copy), the item is tagged `[ASSUMPTION]`. Gaps it found in upstream documents are tagged `[NOTE FOR ARCHITECTURE]` or `[NOTE FOR UX]` and gathered in Open Items. If this spine conflicts with a mock or wireframe, the spine wins.

## Foundation

- **Form factor.** Desktop web, one surface. Local mode runs in Chrome on Luis's M1 Mac. Hosted mode runs in any current desktop Chrome, Safari or Firefox at 1280 px or wider (NFR-10, A-15). The layout is designed at 1440 x 900, scales down to 1024 px, and below 1024 px shows the Narrow-screen message (see Responsive & Platform). Photos come from the phone's camera roll as JPEG files. There is no native mobile client and no live phone capture (§9).
- **No UI system.** The client is vanilla TypeScript. Scenes and Hotspots are three.js textured planes with polygon Hotspots (AD-11). Client types are generated from `GET /schema` (AD-2). Because nothing is inherited, this spine specifies all behavior and `DESIGN.md` specifies every token and component.
- **Visual reference.** `DESIGN.md` defines the Lamplit study direction: warm dark chrome, parchment for cards, serif headings, and the player's own photos framed like pictures in a wizard's study (on the Play screen the Scene is the one framed thing, reviewer feedback Oct 29; in story mode the Riddle note is a parchment note pinned to the wall beside the photo, and the pack and the Treasure chest stand on the floor either side of it). Every component named below is a `DESIGN.md.Components` row with the same name. The four key-screen mocks the builder chose are linked from the surface map rows they illustrate (Capture screen, Greeting / First-room welcome, Play screen, Completion screen, Build view). They show this spine and `DESIGN.md` at work and add nothing to them; the `DESIGN.md` tables stay the visual spec.
- **Theme and senses.** Dark theme only. No sound in v1, except the intro's music, heard only once the player turns it on with "Sound on" (owner exception, Oct 29: browsers block audible autoplay, so the intro always starts muted). Motion is slow and warm, with no transition over 300 ms and no looping animation, with three exceptions. The Hosted landing's chooser (owner decision Oct 29, DESIGN.md Motion): its three cards are dealt in, breathe, lift, tilt and turn over, dust drifts in the lamp light, its card clip and the sample room's photos loop, and a Pause motion control holds it all still. And the full-screen intro (owner decision Oct 29, DESIGN.md Motion): the owner's ten-second teaser, played once over the whole page on the first visit to the Hosted landing and faded out over 600 ms. And the Play screen's walk and story (owner decision Oct 29, story mode, DESIGN.md Motion): turning swings the neighbouring views round, a doorway pushes through to the next area, and at rest the photo pushes in slowly and drifts with the pointer; every one of them is a cut, or still, under reduced motion. Reduced-motion settings are honored everywhere (NFR-14). Input is mouse and keyboard. English only `[ASSUMPTION]`. No browser notifications and no offline mode: losing the network shows the reconnecting state only, on the Capture and Play screens alike (State Patterns).
- **Lead with what the room knows, never with photorealism (PRD §7).** The UI shows off what the Architect read, not how pretty the photo is:
  - The Mask preview labels every found object on a parchment chip in `{typography.label}`, and the Architect's line names two or three of them before giving the count.
  - The Capture screen's `done` state and the Build view lead with the found objects and their outlines; every Build view overlay carries its label.
  - On the Riddler sample page, the Evidence cards, with the attributes the Architect read, sit directly under the Clue they support. In play (story mode) no evidence shows before a lock opens: the clue and the riddle's place tag say where to look.
  - The Scene photo is never enhanced, relit, retouched or upscaled. It is framed, never improved.
- **Helps without telling (UJ-2).** The Guide's help is always visible, always tied to a Puzzle, and never an answer. Every hint is pinned to the Riddle note of the riddle it helps ("The Guide's hint · Nudge"), and the note shows the Active puzzle alone. Every reply to a Hint request visibly arrives and is announced. Rung copy follows the scaffold ladder in Voice and Tone. Story mode (owner decision Oct 29): under the default hint policy, `chest`, help comes only when asked, from the treasure chest's 2 hints for the whole Session (server-enforced, `hints_left` in session state); idle time and wrong attempts open nothing, so no Rung arrives unasked, and an ask opens the next Rung of the Active puzzle at once. (amended 29 Oct 2026, story mode)
- **The client renders server state.** The client renders state and posts events. It never decides whether a lock is open, which Rung is current, or whether a Trickster edit applies (AD-4). It never holds a solution or an edit payload: play routes return `PlayView`, the Build view reads `BuildView`, and Cards reach it as `cards` in session state, the `TricksterCard` projection (AD-3).
- **Two modes, one product.** The HTTP surface is the same in both modes (AD-9). This spine describes Local mode by default; Hosted Mode Differences is the one home for everything that changes in Hosted mode.

### Navigation rules

- Modals are one level deep. The story intro, the Leave room confirmation and the Card log are all modal (scrim below the top bar, focus trapped, Scene, Hotspots and top bar inert) and never open on top of each other.
- **Client routes** `[ASSUMPTION]`: hash routes, because FastAPI serves `web/dist` statically at `/` (AD-9) and this avoids a server-side fallback. The routes are `#/` (Local: Capture screen; Hosted: Hosted landing), `#/build-your-own` (Hosted: the Capture screen an accepted Access code opens, so the browser Back button returns to the landing; with no code held in the tab, as after a reload, and in Local mode, it is `#/` in place, owner report Oct 29), `#/build/<job_id>` (Capture screen for that Build; resumes polling, FR-32), `#/build/<job_id>/view` (Build view), `#/play/<session_id>` (Play screen; reload resumes, FR-11), and `#/done/<session_id>` (Completion screen). The `?profile=` query parameter (AD-14) is carried on every route.
- **Stored Rooms (Local).** The list comes from `GET /rooms` (AD-9); the browser keeps no list of its own. Row behavior is in Component Patterns (Stored Room row). Replaying a stored Room never shows the Timer.
- **Leave room** opens a confirmation. Confirming records no Room result (§8).
- The browser Back button moves between routes. On the Play screen it acts like "Leave room" and asks for the same confirmation `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Every screen has a way back to the start** (owner report Oct 29: "theres no back buttons if they want to exit the build modes or other ones as well"). The top bar's wordmark "Keyframe" is a link to `#/` on every screen, named "Keyframe, back to the start", keyboard reachable with the shared focus ring. On the Play screen it asks the Leave room confirmation first, as "Leave room" does; on the Completion screen only the Play screen's controls are inert, so it goes home at once. The Hosted Capture screen (`#/build-your-own`) has "Back to the three cards" at the top of its left column; the Build progress screen (`#/build/<job_id>`) has "Back to the start" there. The other screens already had one: the chooser's backs ("Back", Escape), the Build view ("Back"), the Completion screen ("Build another room"), Session gone ("Start again"), the Deleted Judge room notice and the unknown-room card ("Go to the demo rooms", or "Build a room" in Local mode). The Narrow-screen message has none by design: no control on it leaves the page, and the app returns intact above 1024 px.

## Voice and Tone

Microcopy only. Brand voice and how the two registers look live in `DESIGN.md` (Brand & Style).

### Register rule (PRD §7)

| Register | Lines | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **In character** | Rungs, the No-hint line, the story intro's lines (a returning player's Greeting among them), Cards (the Trickster's sentence), Clues, Lore, Build stages (the Architect's stage lines), each wizard's introduction voice line, the Guide's Completion sign-off | `{typography.wizard-line}` (or `{typography.wizard-line-lg}`, `{typography.lore}`), signed with the speaker's Wizard portrait, usually on parchment. Wry and playful, never condescending, one or two sentences. The Architect observes, the Riddler teases, the Guide encourages, and the Trickster has the most fun. |
| **Plain messages** | Format and count errors, upload failure, `failed`, `needs_more_photos`, `needs_recapture`, `queued`, Spend cap, Build quota, Access code, session start refused, deletion messages, the Retention summary, the Privacy line, the chest's name, count and dialog lines, the pack's captions, the coach pill and "How hints work", the reconnecting state, the mode indicator, the Hosted pipeline line, every caption in the Build view, every Disclosure | `{typography.body-md}` or `{typography.body-sm}`. No portrait, never on parchment, no jokes. Says what happened and what to do next. |
| **Plain labels and chips** | Build stage names and done-row results, Rung names and "Puzzle {n}" labels, evidence counts, Card outcome labels, the "Proposed: …" line and the Validator's reason on a rejected Card, the Timer label, the "Checking", "Not this" and "Solved" chips, stat labels, Hotspot and mask label chips, each wizard's name and role line where it is introduced | `{typography.label}` or `{typography.body-sm}`, sans. May sit on parchment. |

Headings are set in the serif everywhere, on plain surfaces too (`DESIGN.md` Brand & Style); the register is carried by the body face and the portrait, never by the heading.

General rules: sentence case everywhere. No exclamation marks, except the Trickster's trap line in the story, "Oh no, you seem to be trapped!" (owner call, amended 29 Oct 2026, story mode). No emoji. No "Oops". No "successfully". A line never names a solution token, and wizard lines never name an object unless they are a Clue, an Evidence card, a Rung pointing where to look, or the Architect's line naming what it found. Numbers are digits. A state never relies on color or an icon alone: each one carries a word.

### Helps without telling: Rung and Guide line rules (PRD §7, §3 Rung)

| Level | Says | Length | Sample `[ASSUMPTION]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nudge | Where to look | Shortest: one short sentence | "The shelf has been very quiet. Suspiciously quiet." (PRD §7 sample) |
| Direction | What to notice there | Longer than the nudge | "Those spines aren't all facing the same way, and I doubt that's an accident." |
| Near-answer | How to use what was noticed | Longest, still at most two sentences | "Read the spines that face out, left to right, and let them tell you the order." |

- **Scaffold, never answer.** No Rung says "the answer is", "the code is" or anything like it, and none names a solution token beyond the Rule 5 allowance. Each Rung is enough on its own to move the player one step forward.
- **Rungs increase in length** from nudge to near-answer.
- **After the near-answer**, a further Hint request repeats the near-answer (A-21). There is no reveal or skip.
- **No-hint line criteria (PRD §7):** in-world and encouraging; never a refusal and never "no hint available"; it acknowledges the ask, says the Guide is watching and help is coming, invites one more look, and holds nothing about any Puzzle. The PRD sample, "Look around a little longer; I'll speak up if you stay stuck.", invites a look and promises help but does not clearly acknowledge the ask, so the fixed line adds a short acknowledgement: "A fair ask. Look around a little longer; I'll speak up if you stay stuck." The builder accepted this on two conditions, which the line meets:
  - **No clue.** The acknowledgement names no object, place, attribute or Puzzle content, and neither does the rest of the line. It says nothing about what was typed, because nothing typed is read.
  - **No interruption.** It arrives as an ordinary Rung line in the Hint area and is announced through the polite live region. No modal, no focus move, no scrim, and no input is blocked or delayed: the Scene, the Wizard notes and the lock inputs stay usable while it shows.
- **Story mode (the chest):** the Guide no longer speaks up on its own, so the "I'll speak up" promise would be false. Under the chest the No-hint line is "A fair ask. That one is all I had for this riddle; look once more."; it answers only an ask that opened no Rung (the near-answer already open, or no Puzzle active) and spends no hint. An ask with no hint left is refused by the server with the plain "The chest is empty." and the client shows the empty chest. The Trickster's refused `loosen` reads "Hints come only from the chest." and its refused `decoy` "Evidence is hidden until a lock opens." (amended 29 Oct 2026, story mode)

### Voice for generated lines

Most of the wry voice lives in lines the models write: Clues, Lore and Rungs (authored by the Riddler at Build time) and the Greeting and phrased Rungs (phrased locally by the Guide model). Hosted mode and every fallback show the authored lines verbatim, so they must already be in voice. The prompts carry these rules (AD-5): the Riddler author prompt carries the generated-line rules (Clue and Lore in the Riddler's voice, Rungs in the Guide's voice on the scaffold ladder, no solution tokens), and the `phrase` and `greet` fixed prefixes carry "reword, never add content" and the Greeting brief.

| Line | Written by | Voice | Length | Sample `[ASSUMPTION]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clue | Riddler prompt | The Riddler's: teasing, points at the Evidence without quoting it | One or two sentences | "Someone in this room is very proud of the year they were made. Ask the mug." For an order Puzzle: "Some books on this shelf are better dressed than the rest." |
| Lore | Riddler prompt | The Riddler's, in-world flavor; no help, no tokens | At most 60 words | "The mug has outlived three kettles and one diet. It remembers everything and says nothing, unless you look closely." |
| Rungs (nudge, direction, near-answer) | Riddler prompt, written in the Guide's voice because the Guide signs them | The Guide's: encouraging, warm; the scaffold ladder above | As the ladder | See the ladder above |
| Phrased Rung | Guide model (Local) | The Guide's | No longer than the Rung it rewords | Rewords; never adds content |
| Greeting | Guide model (Local) or the template | Wry, warm and specific about play style (PRD §7) | One or two sentences | See the template below |
| Story lines (amended 29 Oct 2026, story mode; 30 Oct) | Storyteller prompt (`muse-spark-1.3`, once per Build, the story stage) | Each wizard's own, per the attitudes below | One or two short sentences, at most 160 characters | See Story lines below |

- **Per-wizard attitude.** The Architect observes (plain facts with a dry aside), the Riddler teases (never mocks), the Guide encourages (never scolds, never sighs), the Trickster has the most fun (mischief aimed at the room, never at the player). Never condescending.
- **Story lines (amended 29 Oct 2026, story mode).** Each Room's wizard lines are written at build time from a brief that holds only the Room's name, its area labels, the counts of photos, things and riddles and the labels of the scenery objects (those no Puzzle's solution or evidence names): never a solution, a target or item label, an evidence attribute or a code (a scenery label holding a word of those is left out of the brief, unless the word is in the room's name or area labels). The prompt gives each wizard a personality (the Architect proud and precise, the Trickster gleeful and mischievous, the Riddler arch and teasing, the Guide warm and dry) with the fixed lines below and the wizard voice lines as style examples per slot, and asks for short, concrete, wry lines in plain words, each naming at most one scenery object. A line that names any of those, says "AI", "model" or "API", runs past 160 characters, is empty, carries an exclamation mark (bar the trap line) or uses filler ("drift", "linger", "sway", "gently", "softly", in any form) is sent back once with its fixed reasons, and so is a Guide's chest line that does not say how many hints the chest holds (2), a Riddler's entrance that does not say how many riddles there are (the number in digits or in words; a count's word is no spoiler, a code's digits stay refused), an exit riddle's solved line that does not say the way out is open, and a visitor's welcome that thanks the visitor for photos (a word that is also in the room's name or one of its area labels never counts as naming an answer, since the player already sees those: "your front hall" passes in a Room whose way out is the "double front door", while "the door" is still refused; amended 30 Oct 2026, story mode); if the second answer still breaks a rule, the story keeps every line of the last usable answer that passes (the second when it parses with one riddle per Puzzle, else the first) and fills only the refused slots from the fixed lines below, filled from the Room (a refused riddle line takes that riddle's fixed line); with no usable answer, or when the call fails, all the fixed lines stand, and the Build goes on. The exit is only ever "the way out", never named by what it is, and no line names anything that might be locked, hidden or carried. The fixed lines name no object and write the riddle and hint counts in words, so each passes these checks. Slots and their fixed lines (30 Oct: the visitor's welcome added): the Architect's welcome, in the player's own Room, "Thank you for your photos. I built your {areas} from them: {photos} angles, {things} things worth a closer look." ({areas} "room" when the photos name none); the Architect's welcome to a visitor in someone else's Room (a Demo room), never thanking them for photos, "Welcome to someone's {areas}. I built it from their photos: {photos} angles, {things} things worth a closer look."; the Trickster's trap, "Oh no, you seem to be trapped! I locked the way out and gave the key to the Riddler. Wicked of me, I know."; the Riddler's entrance, "Don't mind him; he thinks a locked room is a party. To escape, you must answer my riddles: {riddles} of them, one at a time."; the Guide's chest line, "Stuck? I keep {hints} hints for the whole house. Choose your moment." ({hints} 2); per riddle, the Riddler's opening, "You must answer this.", and its solved line by lock type ("Well found. Keep hold of that; this house isn't finished with it." for a find whose target a later use_on carries, else "Well found. The house noticed you noticing."; use_on "The right thing in the right place. The house approves."; code "The numbers agree with you. They rarely agree with anyone."; order "In order at last. Someone here will be thrilled."; the exit "Every riddle, every answer. The way out is yours."); at the escape, the Trickster's "Fine. Out you go. I'll lock it again for the next guest.", the Riddler's "Clever mind. I'll write harder ones next time." and the Guide's sign-off with no hint spent, "Not a single hint. I'm almost offended.", or with one, "You let me help a little. I enjoyed every word.". The client adds the where-line to an opening and a solve line. A Room built before story mode serves no story, and the client fills the same lines. A Demo room's stored story can have single lines replaced by the Demo room patch (`scripts/patch_demo_room.py`, the ops `patch-demo-room` action), each line checked by these same per-line rules before anything is written; on the Demo room the Guide's chest line is the fixed one, "Stuck? I keep two hints for the whole house. Choose your moment.", since the model's "Stuck, I keep two hints…" read as if the Guide were stuck (amended 30 Oct 2026, story mode).
- **Phrasing may reword but never add content.** A phrased Rung or Greeting carries no fact that is not in its source (the Rung text or the Derived block numbers): no new object, no new hint, no solution, no play history beyond the Derived block.
- **Never name a token.** No Clue, Lore line or Rung names a solution token beyond the Rule 5 allowance, and no Evidence card displays one. Validator rule 5 enforces this on the Rungs, the Clue and `evidence.visible_attribute` with the same matching rules: every displayed attribute says where to look and never holds a solution token (Schema v1, AD-3).

**Greeting and First-room welcome (Guide, in character)**

- **Greeting brief (PRD §7).** Wry, warm and specific about play style; never about objects; never surveillance-flavored; one or two sentences. It should make a returning player feel recognized by the game, not tracked by a service.
- **Greeting template.** Used when phrasing is unavailable: cut 3, Ollama past 5 s, or Ollama unreachable. AD-5's "template filled with the numbers" is realized as one template whose phrase is chosen by the Derived block's numbers `[ASSUMPTION]`, checked in this order:
  - `time_to_first_hint_s` is null (the player never asked): "Welcome back. Last time you never asked me once; let's see what this room makes of you."
  - `persistence` ≥ 0.34: "Welcome back. Last time you kept going long past my first nudge; let's see what this room makes of you."
  - `persistence` −0.33 to 0.33: "Welcome back. Last time you held out a good while before asking; let's see what this room makes of you."
  - `persistence` ≤ −0.34: "Welcome back. Last time you held out just long enough to get your bearings before asking; let's see what this room makes of you."
  With Ollama, `greet(derived)` phrases the line freely under the Greeting brief and the generated-line rules: no object, no solution, nothing that sounds like surveillance, nothing beyond the Derived block.
- **First-room welcome.** "Welcome to your own room. Read a riddle, then click the thing it means." **Demo room variant**, chosen by the server for a Session on a Demo room (which is not the player's room): "Welcome to someone else's room. Read a riddle, then click the thing it means." `[ASSUMPTION]` (see Open Items). Both are two short sentences that say what to do, so they wrap cleanly on the 560 px card (changed Oct 29, owner decision after a playtest: the PRD §7 phrase "Find what doesn't belong" misled first-time players, since every puzzle is find-the-object-the-riddle-means). **How to play**, on the First-room welcome only (a Greeting's player has played before), three numbered plain steps between the line and the voice line: 1. In story mode (owner decision Oct 29) the play screen shows neither the welcome line, its steps nor its tip: the story intro opens every Session (Story intro above), and the coach pill teaches the first moves.

## Interaction Primitives

- **Click** to act everywhere. A Hotspot click always posts `click` (AD-4), with one exception: the target click of a select-then-click, which posts only the `use_on` lock input. A click is never by itself a Wrong attempt; the only click-driven Wrong attempt is a full-length, non-matching armed order sequence (Component Patterns, Order click progress).
- **Hover** reveals a Hotspot (`{components.hotspot}` hover state, 150 ms) and names it after a 400 ms dwell. Hover is never the only path: Tab focus and "Show all objects" reveal the same Hotspots (NFR-14).
- **Keyboard.** Tab and Shift+Tab move in reading order: top bar → the Riddle note's lore disclosure (and its lock input) → Scene Hotspots → doorway sign → turn discs → the pack's items → the Treasure chest → walk map. Enter acts as a click on Hotspots, the walk's buttons, Inventory items, a Demo room card's "Play this room" and graph nodes. Enter submits the Code field and the Access code field. Escape closes the Card log and the chest's dialog (focus back to the chest), else cancels a `use_on` selection ("Hands empty."), means "Keep playing" on the Leave room confirmation, and turns the Hosted landing's chosen card home. Escape never dismisses the story intro; "Skip intro" ends it. On the Hosted landing the arrow keys, Home and End move focus between the three cards, a roving group, not a shortcut (owner decision Oct 29). On the Play screen ArrowLeft and ArrowRight turn and ArrowUp goes through the doorway (story mode, owner decision Oct 29), from the Scene, a button or the page, never from a field, a scrolling tray or the chest's dialog and never while the Scene is held inert under an overlay or a beat. Enter anywhere presses a beat's button. No other single-key shortcuts in v1 `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Drag** (`use_on`): pointer drag from the Inventory onto a Hotspot. **Select-then-click** is the equivalent for keyboard and pointer: select the item, optionally switch Scenes, then click or Enter on a Hotspot. The two paths are identical to the server (AD-4, AD-11); Component Patterns (`use_on` drag, `use_on` select-then-click) holds their rules.
- **Active puzzle.** AD-4 defines it as the most recently targeted available unsolved Puzzle (targeted by a lock input, or by a Hint request naming a Puzzle), else the earliest available unsolved Puzzle; it moves on its own when its Puzzle opens (a `find` Puzzle opened by a click included) or a Merge hides it, it is null only once the Exit has opened, and clicks never set it (an exploration click leaves it unchanged). Session state exposes it as `active_puzzle_id`; the client reads it and never computes it, and always omits `puzzle_id` from a Hint request. The Riddle note shows it alone and the chest's hint is pinned there (amended 29 Oct 2026, correct-course record; story mode).
- **Order sequence, code submit, Hint request.** Each has one home in Component Patterns: Order click progress, Code field, and Treasure chest. The order sequence is armed only while its Puzzle is the Active puzzle (AD-4): when another Puzzle becomes Active, the pips reset and the control returns to "Start the sequence"; the player re-arms with it.
- **Polling.** Session every 2000 ms (AD-4); Build every 2000 ms `[ASSUMPTION]`. The client never stamps time: `t_ms` comes from the API.
- **Refetch on `room_revision`.** When the polled `room_revision` changes, the client fetches `GET /sessions/<id>/room` and re-renders the Riddle note and Hotspot set from the new `PlayView` (AD-12).
- **Trickster request.** When polled state shows an open Trickster window, the client posts `POST /sessions/<id>/trickster` once, with no visible pending state `[ASSUMPTION]`, and never again while the state shows the window `pending`; the server makes one source call per window and answers a repeat with the unchanged state (AD-4). The Card arrives in the reply and through the poll, as a new entry in `cards`.
- **Banned:** sliding, bouncing or shaking (a Wrong attempt is play, not failure); looping animation of any kind (the Hosted landing's chooser alone excepted, owner decision Oct 29, DESIGN.md Motion); sound (the intro's music alone excepted, heard only once the player turns it on, owner decision Oct 29); browser notifications; an offline mode; free conversation with any wizard; a reveal or skip after the near-answer (A-21); highlighting a new Evidence card; dismissing a Disclosure; modals more than one level deep; text over a photo except on a label chip; any image generation in play (FR-23).

## Accessibility Floor

Behavioral; contrast lives in `DESIGN.md` (Colors, contrast table). The floor is NFR-14 and AD-11, exactly:

1. Every Hotspot is reachable by Tab and shows a visible focus ring; Enter acts as a click.
2. A "Show all objects" toggle outlines every Hotspot.
3. A `use_on` input can be given by select-then-click and posts the same `UseOnInput` as a drop.
4. Text meets WCAG AA contrast on the dark theme.
5. Rungs, Cards and the Greeting are announced through one polite live region.
6. Motion respects reduced-motion settings.

How the floor is realized (behavior, not new requirements):

- **Focus.** Each Hotspot is a focusable element in the three.js scene's accessible overlay (DOM-only fallback: the same ids, AD-11). The focus ring is traced along the polygon, and the label chip gives the visual name for as long as the Hotspot has focus.
- **Show all objects.** The toggle is in the top bar, pressed state announced. It persists across Scene changes within the Session `[ASSUMPTION]`. It draws outlines only, with no labels and no fill (`{colors.hotspot-show-all}`).
- **Live region.** There is one region (`aria-live="polite"`), and only hints ("The Guide's hint, nudge: {text}"; the No-hint line goes out as the feedback line), Cards, the Trickster's chest line, each beat's spoken line (a returning player's Greeting among them), the Guide's Completion sign-off, the reconnecting line, the feedback line, the Narrow-screen message's copied-link confirmation, the Completion screen's copied-result confirmation, the Hosted landing's card announcement (once the pointer has settled on a card for 500 ms, never for the same card twice in a row; keyboard focus is announced by the card's own name) and the walk's view description after every move (story mode, Voice and Tone) are written to it. Every in-character line is prefixed with its speaker ("The Guide:", "The Trickster:"). Cards read "The Trickster: {sentence} {outcome label}." (the label names the Validator); a rejected Card appends its "Proposed: …" line and plain reason ("That change was refused. {fixed message}"), so the UJ-3 climax is not lost. The first Card of the Session reads "The Trickster. {role line} {voice line} {sentence} {outcome label}.", so the introduction is heard once. Announcement rules:
  - **Feedback line** `[ASSUMPTION: an addition above the floor]`: each reply's feedback line is written once, as "The Guide: {line}" ("The Guide: That doesn't go there.", "The Guide: You pick up the throw pillows. Riddle 1 solved."), so a screen-reader player hears every verdict without moving focus; the "Not this · n" and "Solved" chips are not written again. "Checking" is not announced.
  - **Replies to explicit Hint requests are always announced** (Component Patterns, Treasure chest).
  - **Rungs and Cards that arrive through the poll** are announced once each.
  - **Nothing is re-announced on reload.**
  - **The story intro** writes each beat's line to the live region once, as "{name}: {line}", the last beat's with "Riddle 1 of {N}: {clue}" after it; a returning player's Greeting is read there as the Guide's line.
- **Build view keyboard.** Initial focus on the header title. Tab then moves through "Back", the Model table, the In play block, the verdict table and Dry run rows, the graph nodes, the mask label chips and the JSON toggle (the right column first, matching its place above the fold, then the left) `[ASSUMPTION]`. Tables are real `<table>` elements with header cells `[ASSUMPTION]`.
- **Card log.** A modal dialog: focus trapped, the Scene, Hotspots and top bar inert, Escape closes it and focus returns to its opener (Component Patterns).
- **The Hosted landing's back panel.** A modal dialog (`role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`) named by the chosen card's title: focus moves into it, Tab and Shift+Tab cycle its visible controls, everything outside it is inert, and Escape (from any control but the video's own native controls, which keep their keys), "Back" or a click on the bare stage turns it home, focus returning to the card (State Patterns).
- **The intro.** A modal dialog (`role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`) named "Keyframe intro" over the whole page: focus moves to "Skip intro" ("Enter Keyframe" at its end), Tab and Shift+Tab cycle its visible controls, everything behind it is inert, Escape skips or enters, and focus goes back where it was when it closes (State Patterns).
- **Order click progress** carries a visually hidden "{filled} of {length}" description; the "Start the sequence" control is a plain button whose label changes to "Stop the sequence" while armed (a label change, not a pressed state), and keeps focus across the change.
- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`): every fade becomes instant (a repeated Hint reply is still announced); the arrival glow becomes the lit ring held for 2 s with no animation, as do the unlock moment's Hotspot bloom and Scene frame glow (the lit fill and rule held 2 s) while the Solved chip and the feedback line appear at once; the 300 ms wrong flash on the order pips becomes an instant reset; the Hosted landing's chooser deals and turns nothing, each change a 160 ms crossfade, its dust holds still, its card clip waits on its poster with no toggle and its photos do not cycle (State Patterns); the Completion stats show their final values at once, with no count; on the Play screen every turn and push-in is a cut, with no blur, no slow push-in at rest and no pointer drift (story mode). The Guide thinking state, the Pending chip's ellipsis and the Timer's lamp dot are static in every setting.
- **Contrast.** Every text and state pair used here is listed in the `DESIGN.md` contrast table. No state depends on color alone.
